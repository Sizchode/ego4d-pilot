# Partial cross-model failure mining

**Status: partial evidence collected while the VGGT, π³-X, and MapAnything main jobs are still running.** All four backbones have completed the same 30 HiRoom, 11 ETH3D and 11 ScanNet++ scenes, while the remaining configurations are incomplete. This note does not select a toolkit or assign failure labels. It audits 13 HiRoom scenes through 51 archived backbone-case bundles, adds 16 audited bundles for four representative ETH3D scenes and 44 audited bundles for the current ScanNet++ common population, and reports threshold-free common-population statistics using official DA3-BENCH metrics and the same camera-error protocol.

The machine-readable extraction is `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/summary.csv`. It is generated by `scripts/failure_mining/summarize_case_bundles.py`, which reads each run's `validation.json` and `camera_pairs.npz` without thresholds or model-specific branches. The local archive is covered by `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/archive_manifest.json`: 51 completed, validation-passed case bundles across 13 scenes, with all 408 required files recorded by byte size and exact SHA-256. The manifest itself has SHA-256 recorded in the local run manifest.

## Case 1: 828812 is a diagnostic false positive, not a reconstruction failure

This is a textured living-room sequence with eleven views. All three completed backbones have strong official pose and geometry scores:

| Backbone | Pose AUC@30 ↑ | Reconstruction F-score ↑ | Translation error p95 ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DA3 | 0.997 | 0.934 | 1.16° |
| VGGT | 0.960 | 0.813 | 3.97° |
| π³-X | 0.977 | 0.893 | 2.85° |

The original DA3 projectable consistency diagnostic nevertheless ranked this scene worst among the 30 HiRoom scenes: relative-depth residual p95 was 1.523 and round-trip residual p95 was 241.5 pixels. Once source points hidden behind the target depth were removed by the deterministic z-buffer screen, the relative-depth p95 fell to 0.0215. The official accuracy stayed strong throughout.

This provides a concrete negative result for naive failure mining: projectability is not visibility. A large cross-view residual can be caused by occlusion and disocclusion even when camera and geometry metrics are good. It cannot be used as an error detector without visibility handling.

Evidence: `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/{da3,vggt,pi3x}-828812/`; full DA3 visibility analysis is in `da3-main-results-failure-mining.md`.

## Case 2: 828824 separates pose headroom from geometry collapse

The thirteen views are dominated by white walls, ceilings, curtains, and close-up planar surfaces with little stable texture. DA3, VGGT and π³-X all place the sequence near the bottom of the 30-scene HiRoom pose ranking. MapAnything obtains a better trajectory but substantially worse dense geometry:

| Backbone | Pose AUC@30 ↑ | Reconstruction F-score ↑ | Translation error p95 ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DA3 | 0.706 | 0.632 | 37.35° |
| VGGT | 0.506 | 0.321 | 84.64° |
| π³-X | 0.781 | 0.567 | 22.42° |
| MapAnything | 0.873 | 0.206 | 11.04° |

The camera-error matrices show errors distributed across many view pairs rather than one corrupt frame. VGGT is markedly worse, and recurrence in three separately trained reconstruction backbones makes a model-specific pose implementation bug less likely. Their rankings of the 78 pairwise translation errors are strongly correlated (Spearman 0.771–0.849). MapAnything's pair ranking is weakly negative against each of them (-0.179 to -0.218), consistent with its recovery of a different trajectory rather than a uniform rescaling of the same errors. Its low F-score and visibly unstable depth nevertheless prevent “use another backbone” from being a complete recovery. The defensible category is therefore **weak-evidence multiview input with separable pose and dense-geometry failures**, rather than one four-backbone pose failure.

This case also exposes the opposite diagnostic problem from 828812. DA3's visibility-aware relative-depth p95 is only 0.0312 even though the GT pose and geometry scores are poor. The predicted cameras and depths can therefore be mutually consistent while jointly wrong in the benchmark coordinate geometry. A self-consistency check alone can miss this failure.

Evidence: `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/{da3,vggt,pi3x,mapanything}-828824/`.

## Case 3: 828760 exposes both shared difficulty and cross-backbone headroom

This ten-view bedroom sequence contains large blank walls and ceilings, repeated room corners, curtains, an overexposed window, and several close-up views with little texture. It is the most consistently poor pose case among the 30 scenes shared by DA3, VGGT and π³-X. MapAnything has also completed this scene, giving a four-backbone case audit:

| Backbone | Pose AUC@30 ↑ | Reconstruction F-score ↑ | Translation error p95 ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DA3 | 0.749 | 0.676 | 31.79° |
| VGGT | 0.221 | 0.077 | 84.42° |
| π³-X | 0.713 | 0.675 | 32.31° |
| MapAnything | 0.896 | 0.490 | 8.83° |

Three models produce severe translation-direction errors, with VGGT collapsing almost completely. MapAnything recovers a substantially better trajectory on the same images, so the observations are not wholly uninformative and the poor result is not an unavoidable property of the sequence. Its geometry remains weak, however: better pose does not translate into the best F-score. This separates two possible recovery targets—camera estimation and depth/fusion quality—and warns against treating “switch backbone” as a complete repair.

Per-view aggregation localizes a shared weak point. View 8 has the largest median translation-direction error in all four models: 12.78° for DA3, 38.93° for VGGT, 14.62° for π³-X and 6.63° for MapAnything. Its original image is dominated by an overexposed window and a close curtain. View 4, which shows mostly a blank ceiling and wall junction, is also among the three highest-error views for DA3, VGGT and MapAnything and is tied near the top for π³-X. These observations give a concrete image-conditioned diagnosis candidate, but do not yet distinguish a VLM from saturation, texture-coverage, feature-count or parallax baselines.

A first threshold-free image check supports that caution. View 8 has the lowest luminance entropy of the sequence (6.735 bits) and the largest all-channel ceiling ratio (0.197). Across the ten views, luminance entropy has Spearman correlation -0.729 with DA3 median translation error and -0.590 with VGGT, but only -0.170 for π³-X and -0.152 for MapAnything. Gradient and clipping statistics are likewise inconsistent across models. This is a ten-view descriptive result, not a significance claim; it shows that simple image quality may explain part of the failure but is not yet a model-independent detector.

DA3's original relative-depth p95 is only 0.0595; after visibility screening it is 0.0188, despite AUC30 0.749 and translation p95 31.79°. Like 828824, this is an internally consistent but externally inaccurate state. The stronger MapAnything pose makes 828760 especially useful for later testing whether observable state can predict which backbone or pose hypothesis is preferable. That intervention is not yet selected as a tool.

Evidence: `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/{da3,vggt,pi3x,mapanything}-828760/`; per-view statistics are in `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/view_summary.csv`; image statistics and correlations are in `inputs-828760/image_evidence.csv` and `image_pose_correlations.csv`.

## Case 4: 828761 is shared pose difficulty but not shared geometry failure

This twelve-view bedroom sequence repeatedly observes the same walls, window, curtains and furniture from nearby viewpoints. Every backbone places it among its seven worst HiRoom scenes by pose AUC@30, although the absolute pose scores differ substantially:

| Backbone | Pose AUC@30 ↑ | HiRoom failure rank | Reconstruction F-score ↑ | Translation error p95 ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DA3 | 0.958 | 6 / 30 | 0.872 | 4.07° |
| VGGT | 0.825 | 4 / 30 | 0.913 | 16.74° |
| π³-X | 0.917 | 7 / 30 | 0.587 | 11.11° |
| MapAnything | 0.654 | 1 / 30 | 0.191 | 29.77° |

The error is not isolated to one universally bad frame. Views 7 and 8 have the largest median translation errors for VGGT; view 8 is worst for MapAnything and second-worst for π³-X; DA3 spreads much smaller errors across views 3, 4, 7 and 8. More generally, all six cross-model comparisons over the same 66 view pairs have positive translation-error rank correlation, from 0.548 to 0.853. The same view relationships are therefore repeatedly difficult even though the error magnitude differs by model. This pattern is compatible with weak translation constraints from repeated, nearby observations, but it does not establish the cause. A parallax or correspondence-degeneracy measurement is required before assigning a failure label.

Dense geometry behaves differently. VGGT has the best F-score despite its poor pose rank, DA3 is also strong, π³-X is moderate, and MapAnything is weak. The scene is therefore a common pose-difficulty candidate, not a universal reconstruction collapse. It reinforces that pose and geometry must remain separate targets and that failure mining cannot use one scene-level label.

Evidence: `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/{da3,vggt,pi3x,mapanything}-828761/`; ranks are in `artifacts/partial-results-snapshots/snapshot-04/four_model_hiroom_scene_ranks.csv`; pair-level comparisons are in `artifacts/partial-cross-model-cases/pair_error_rank_agreement.csv`.

## Thirty-scene HiRoom comparison

The shared 30-scene population gives a less case-specific result. Spearman correlations between within-model failure ranks are:

| Metric | DA3–VGGT | DA3–π³-X | DA3–MapAnything | VGGT–π³-X | VGGT–MapAnything | π³-X–MapAnything |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pose AUC@30 | 0.449 | 0.441 | 0.357 | 0.738 | 0.410 | 0.456 |
| Reconstruction F-score | 0.096 | 0.086 | 0.501 | 0.212 | 0.101 | -0.153 |

Pose difficulty is moderately shared, especially between VGGT and π³-X. Dense-geometry rankings are mostly model-specific: DA3–MapAnything is the one moderate positive association, while π³-X–MapAnything is weakly negative. The strongest common pose candidate is 828761, whose minimum four-model worstness fraction is 0.793. The strongest common F-score candidate is 828824, whose minimum worstness fraction is 0.690. The final taxonomy should therefore separate pose failure from depth/fusion failure instead of assigning one scene-level “reconstruction difficulty” label. These correlations remain provisional because they cover HiRoom only.

Within a single backbone, pose and dense-geometry failure ranks are related but not interchangeable. Their Spearman correlations are 0.624 for DA3, 0.580 for VGGT, 0.475 for π³-X and 0.397 for MapAnything. Camera errors explain part of dense reconstruction quality, but substantial endpoint-specific variation remains. Diagnosis, actions and acceptance tests must therefore keep camera recovery separate from depth/fusion recovery instead of optimizing one scene-level failure score.

Machine-readable evidence: `artifacts/partial-results-snapshots/snapshot-04/four_model_hiroom_scene_ranks.csv`, `four_model_hiroom_pairwise_rank_agreement.csv` and `four_model_hiroom_scene_spread.csv`; within-model endpoint correlations are in `artifacts/partial-cross-model-pose-geometry-hiroom/metric_rank_agreement.csv`.

## Eleven-scene ETH3D comparison

Snapshot 05 adds the complete 11-scene ETH3D population for all four backbones. Unlike HiRoom, ETH3D failure ordering is strongly shared. Pairwise pose AUC@30 failure-rank correlations range from 0.509 to 0.818; F-score correlations range from 0.645 to 0.909. This recurrence makes ETH3D the stronger source of candidates for reconstruction recovery rather than model-specific fallback.

Backbone selection has limited headroom on this population. DA3 is the best fixed model for both endpoints. A per-scene, ground-truth oracle raises mean pose AUC@30 from 0.9110 to 0.9203 (mean gain 0.0093; positive gain on 3/11 scenes) and mean F-score from 0.7848 to 0.7884 (mean gain 0.0036; positive gain on 2/11). Median gain is zero for both. Therefore the main ETH3D opportunity is unlikely to be “choose another feed-forward backbone”; it is to recover errors that multiple backbones share.

`delivery_area` is the clearest shared dense-geometry candidate. Every backbone ranks it worst by F-score: DA3 0.440, VGGT 0.112, π³-X 0.256 and MapAnything 0.161. Completeness error is also large for all four (1.107, 1.503, 1.586 and 2.268 respectively), so the result is not merely one model's noisy surface.

Other cases separate action types. `playground` has poor pose across all four (AUC@30 0.772–0.861) but tightly clustered F-score (0.700–0.740); π³-X supplies only +0.0167 F-score oracle gain, so switching models does not remove the shared difficulty. `terrains` has real pose-selection headroom—MapAnything reaches 0.916 versus DA3 0.862—but DA3 retains the best F-score at 0.893; better pose and better geometry again require separate acceptance tests. `relief_2` is consistently near the failure tail, although DA3 remains much stronger than VGGT and MapAnything.

Image-only statistics were then tested directly against per-view camera error. Within each of 32 scene × backbone × rotation/translation endpoints, 21 gradient, luminance, entropy and saturation fields were correlated with incident pair-error p95; Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied within each endpoint. There are 155 significant rows among 664 tests, but the fields are strongly correlated and the direction is scene-dependent, so this is not 155 independent predictors. For example, translation error rises with ceiling saturation in `terrains` for all four backbones (ρ 0.46–0.59), while the same saturation fields are negatively correlated with translation error in `delivery_area` for three backbones (ρ −0.45 to −0.52). In `relief_2`, lower gradient predicts translation difficulty for DA3, π³-X and MapAnything (ρ −0.67 to −0.78), whereas VGGT's much larger translation failure has no image field surviving correction. Most notably, no tested image scalar explains DA3's severe `terrains` rotation outlier (minimum corrected q = 0.867).

These results establish that exposure and texture statistics are useful state features in some sequences, but a single monotonic image-quality rule is not portable across scenes, models and error types. They still do not demonstrate VLM increment. The next baselines must add sequence adjacency, feature coverage and matchability, parallax, pairwise correspondence geometry, deterministic cross-backbone agreement, and depth/fusion diagnostics. A VLM is justified only if it localizes the responsible state or chooses a successful action beyond those baselines under the same tool budget.

Machine-readable evidence is in `artifacts/partial-results-snapshots/snapshot-05`, `snapshot-05-comparison`, and `artifacts/partial-cross-model-eth3d-cases`. The immutable snapshot contains exact hashes of the active batch prefixes and validates every included run before reading its metrics. The ETH3D case archive has SHA-256 recorded in the local run manifest; its 128 required files and 23 selected input-view renders were all verified locally. These conclusions remain provisional until the final 103-scene jobs and dependent audits complete.

Pose failure ordering is moderately to strongly shared: the six pairwise Spearman correlations for AUC@30 range from 0.509 to 0.836. DA3 is the best pose model on all eleven scenes, so a per-scene four-backbone oracle has zero pose-selection gain on this prefix. Geometry is different. DA3 is the best fixed F-score model with mean 0.7726; the per-scene oracle reaches 0.7791, a mean gain of 0.0065 concentrated in 4/11 scenes. MapAnything is a systematic outlier: its pose AUC@30 remains 0.820–0.949, but its mean F-score is 0.2581, versus 0.7726/0.6559/0.7148 for DA3/VGGT/π³-X. Its F-score failure rank is negatively correlated with DA3 (-0.318) and essentially uncorrelated with VGGT (0.009) and π³-X (0.091), even though completeness-error ranks remain substantially shared (Spearman 0.709–0.791 against the other models). This is evidence for a backbone-specific geometry mechanism rather than a universal ScanNet++ scene ordering.

The five newly added scenes sharpen that separation. In `7bc286c1b6`, cross-model pair-error rankings recur moderately for both rotation and translation (median Spearman 0.523 and 0.492), and the four models preserve the same broad ordering in pose and F-score. It is therefore a candidate for shared camera difficulty, although DA3 and π³-X still reach F-scores 0.776 and 0.755. The other four additions do not show a common corrupt-pair pattern: their median pair-error correlations are only 0.115–0.285 for rotation and 0.219–0.386 for translation. In `c5439f4607`, for example, DA3/VGGT/π³-X obtain F-scores 0.825/0.784/0.836 while MapAnything falls to 0.144 despite pose AUC@30 0.914. In `f3d64c30f8`, DA3 and π³-X reach 0.842/0.807, while VGGT and MapAnything reach 0.422/0.270. These are model-specific dense-geometry deficits on observations that other backbones reconstruct well; a universal camera-repair action is not supported by the current evidence.

The evaluator path was audited before assigning a cause. ScanNet++ first aligns predicted camera scale to GT with Umeyama, multiplies the predicted depth by the same scale, resizes the predicted intrinsics, and performs TSDF fusion with the aligned predicted extrinsics. It applies the same GT valid-depth mask to every backbone. It does not read model confidence during fusion. MapAnything's archived configuration also records `apply_confidence_mask=false` and defines the primary branch as official masked `depth_z` plus predicted camera. Therefore the low MapAnything F-score cannot be attributed to its visually flatter confidence maps or a confidence threshold. The remaining coupled explanations are predicted extrinsics, predicted depth, predicted intrinsics, and their interaction under fusion.

Two identical analysis jobs will isolate one of those factors over the eleven-scene population. Slurm job `7679662` covers the first six scenes and job `7680275` covers the five newly completed scenes. Each keeps evaluator-scaled predicted depth and predicted intrinsics fixed, replaces only aligned predicted extrinsics with sampled GT extrinsics, and reruns the identical TSDF and official point-distance computation. These are offline ground-truth interventions (`uses_ground_truth_intervention=true`, `test_time_policy_input=false`), not deployable recovery methods. Their native-mesh resampling controls must first reproduce the captured official metrics closely; otherwise their intervention deltas will not be interpreted. Dependent jobs `7679764` and `7680281` will verify the immutable result streams and report every metric both against the official native result and against the paired same-seed resampling control, without a hand-chosen success threshold.

### Ground-truth-extrinsics intervention: first six scenes

The first 24-case intervention and its audit have completed. The native-mesh, same-seed resampling control exactly reproduces all six captured official metrics in every case: the maximum absolute control deviation is 0 for accuracy, completeness, overall, precision, recall and F-score. The intervention deltas can therefore be interpreted without point-sampling ambiguity.

| Backbone | Native F-score mean | GT-extrinsics mean | Mean change | Improved scenes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DA3 | 0.7529 | 0.7675 | +0.0146 | 6 / 6 |
| VGGT | 0.6752 | 0.6850 | +0.0098 | 3 / 6 |
| π³-X | 0.7035 | 0.7680 | +0.0645 | 5 / 6 |
| MapAnything | 0.2674 | 0.4855 | +0.2180 | 6 / 6 |

For MapAnything, replacing only extrinsics improves both sides of the geometry endpoint in every scene: mean accuracy error falls from 0.0802 to 0.0537, completeness error from 0.2018 to 0.1767, precision rises from 0.3325 to 0.5679, and recall from 0.2241 to 0.4254. Its mean F-score gain closes about 45% of the gap from native MapAnything to native DA3 on this population, but the intervention result remains 0.2674 below native DA3. Extrinsics are therefore a major contributor to MapAnything's collapse, but predicted depth, intrinsics or their fusion interaction still leave substantial headroom.

The intervention is not uniformly beneficial across backbones. VGGT improves on three scenes and worsens on three; its F-score drops by 0.0473 on `1ada7a0617` even with exact GT extrinsics. This is direct evidence that a more accurate camera trajectory does not automatically improve geometry when depth and intrinsics remain fixed. Camera replacement must be accepted on the geometry endpoint separately, and a universal “bad pose → replace/refine pose” action is not justified.

The raw result stream contains 24 cases and has SHA-256 recorded in the local run manifest; the paired case table contains 144 rows and the model-metric table contains 24 rows.

### Ground-truth-extrinsics intervention: eleven-scene conclusion

The five-scene continuation and its audit completed successfully. The two immutable result streams were hash-verified independently and then combined from disjoint case-level rows; no source averages were concatenated. The final population contains 44 model-scene cases and 264 metric rows.

| Backbone | Native/control F-score mean | GT-extrinsics mean | Mean change vs control | Improved / worsened scenes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DA3 | 0.7726 | 0.7891 | +0.0164 | 10 / 1 |
| VGGT | 0.6559 | 0.6771 | +0.0212 | 7 / 4 |
| π³-X | 0.7147 | 0.7892 | +0.0745 | 9 / 2 |
| MapAnything | 0.2581 | 0.4756 | +0.2175 | 11 / 0 |

The resampling control remains close to the captured official metrics. Across the 264 rows, its largest absolute deviation is 0.002204 (VGGT precision on `7bc286c1b6`); for F-score the largest deviations are 0.000400 for VGGT, 0.000555 for π³-X, 0.000029 for DA3 and 0 for MapAnything. The headline mean F-score changes are therefore much larger than control variation. Very small per-scene changes near this control scale are not interpreted by sign.

MapAnything improves in every scene and on both error sides of the reconstruction endpoint: mean accuracy error decreases from 0.0861 to 0.0562, completeness error from 0.1835 to 0.1517, precision rises from 0.3181 to 0.5446, and recall from 0.2180 to 0.4248. Replacing extrinsics closes 42% of the native F-score gap between MapAnything and native DA3, but the intervened mean still trails native DA3 by 0.2970. Camera error is a large, systematic contributor, not a sufficient explanation; depth, intrinsics and fusion compatibility remain unresolved.

π³-X exhibits substantial camera headroom as well: its mean F-score rises by 0.0745, including a +0.2961 change on `5f99900f09`. DA3 gains only 0.0164 on average because its native pose is already strongest on all eleven scenes. VGGT remains the key counterexample: GT extrinsics improve its mean F-score, but four scenes worsen, including −0.0473 on `1ada7a0617` and −0.0332 on `c5439f4607`. This rules out accepting a pose action solely because camera accuracy improves. Any recovery policy must separately predict and verify compatibility with the backbone's depth and intrinsics on the final geometry endpoint.

This intervention identifies an actionable upstream failure axis that GRASE does not modify: camera state inside the reconstruction backbone. It does not yet establish a VLM contribution. The next comparison must test whether observable image-and-state evidence can choose `repair pose`, `repair depth/fusion`, `switch backbone`, or `no action` better than deterministic diagnostics and search under the same tool budget.

Native pose error alone does not provide that decision rule. Over the same eleven scenes, Spearman correlation between native pose AUC@30 and F-score gain from GT extrinsics is −0.591 for π³-X, but +0.600 for MapAnything, +0.282 for VGGT and +0.036 for DA3. Only the π³-X sign matches the simple hypothesis that worse native pose should imply more geometry benefit from camera replacement. MapAnything shows the opposite ordering even though all eleven interventions help. Native geometry F-score is also inconsistent as a gain predictor: its correlations are −0.791/−0.473/−0.391/+0.136 for π³-X/VGGT/DA3/MapAnything. These are descriptive correlations on eleven scenes, not significance claims, but they reject one portable monotonic gate across the four backbones. The eventual camera action must use richer compatibility evidence and retain a geometry-level acceptance check.

## What these cases establish—and what they do not

Together they identify five requirements for the final failure analysis:

1. Visibility-aware diagnostics are needed to reject false positives such as 828812.
2. Relative ranks must remain paired with absolute official metrics and a no-action decision.
3. Backbone selection and ensemble consistency are mandatory baselines for model-specific failures such as 828815 and 828785.
4. Pose recovery and geometry recovery must be evaluated separately: 828760 shows that a better trajectory does not guarantee the best dense geometry, while 828790 shows a geometry deficit despite strong pose.
5. Image evidence beyond reconstruction self-consistency may be needed for jointly wrong states such as 828824, but parallax, feature coverage, exposure and other deterministic visual evidence must be tested first.
6. A camera alarm is not an action policy: GT-camera benefit has backbone-dependent ordering and may leave large depth/intrinsics/fusion error.
7. Additional backbone calls must be budgeted; otherwise weak-source recovery is confounded by trivial model substitution.

The fifth point remains a hypothesis. The audited examples do not prove that a VLM outperforms blur, texture, parallax, confidence or other deterministic image diagnostics. The final analysis must compare all four backbones over all 103 runs, then test those non-VLM diagnostics before claiming a VLM-specific gap.

This is an upstream reconstruction failure: the variables at issue are camera translation and resulting metric geometry. It remains outside GRASE's downstream repair of shell, openings, assets, and object transforms.
